The problem

Content creation is scaling. Brand control is not.

As more people publish more content across more channels, the gaps in brand governance widen. Here is where drafts most often slip through.

50
Claimed in draft
35
Approved in fact sheet
15
Feature gap

Hypothetical exampleA 15-feature gap between what the draft claims and what is approved.

Unsupported claims

Numbers and stats slip into content with no cited source behind them.

Incorrect information

Outdated or wrong product facts get published before anyone notices.

Inconsistent messaging

Tone, names, and phrasing drift across teams and channels.

Unapproved assets

Old logos, off-brand visuals, and retired imagery resurface in drafts.

Accessibility issues

Alt text, contrast, and language checks are easy to miss late in review.

Manual review bottlenecks

Reviewers drown in drafts and checks instead of focusing on judgement.

How it works

From draft to decision in six steps

Every finding is paired with cited evidence, then routed to a person who decides.

1

Ingest

Draft content connects to the review workspace.

2

Analyse

Structure, claims, and naming are parsed.

3

Retrieve

Approved sources are matched for evidence.

4

Explain

Each finding is paired with a cited source.

5

Route

Findings go to the right reviewer, by role.

6

Decide

A human accepts, rejects, or escalates each one.

Privacy

Drafts are reviewed in your workspace. Nothing is sold or repurposed.

Security

Access is role-scoped and decisions are logged for accountability.

Human oversight

Every finding waits for a person to accept, reject, or escalate.

Accessibility

Reviews include contrast, alt text, and plain-language checks.
